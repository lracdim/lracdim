In July 2026, the Philippines' IT-BPM industry body cut its 2028 targets because of AI. In its worst case, headcount slips from 1.89 million workers to 1.85 million. That is the headline people share. The one they skip came from the same body two months later: employment is still projected to grow 2.6% this year, to 1.94 million, while revenue grows faster. On freelance platforms the pattern is sharper. Demand for skills that explicitly mention AI grew 109% on Upwork in a year, against 23% for everything else. And in a study of Filipino virtual assistant job openings, Zapier and n8n showed up in more listings than ChatGPT.

AI is not killing the virtual assistant. It is killing the parts of the job that were only ever typing, and paying more for the parts that need judgment. That is not an ending. It is a redirection, and the VAs who read it early will come out ahead.

## What the numbers show

Three kinds of data, and none of them shows a collapse.

- **The industry is still growing, just with fewer people per dollar.** IBPAP forecasts IT-BPM revenue up 5% to $42.3 billion in 2026 and employment up 2.6% to 1.94 million, with revenue outpacing jobs through 2028. Its revised 2028 range is $43.3 to $50.5 billion in revenue and 1.85 to 2.14 million full-time workers, depending on how fast the industry builds AI capability.
- **Filipino VAs are still the world's largest supply.** VA Masters' State of the Filipino Virtual Assistant Industry 2026 report puts the Philippines at about 38% of the global VA workforce, in a market it sizes at $6.5 billion in 2026.
- **AI skills are where the growth is.** Upwork's In-Demand Skills 2026 report found skills that explicitly reference AI grew 109% year over year, against 23% for the rest. Its Future Workforce Index 2026 found freelancers doing AI work earn 34% more per hour than those who do not.

One caution: most VA data comes from recruitment agencies and platforms that profit from VA hiring, and IBPAP is an industry body. Read the figures as direction, not precision. But the direction is the same everywhere: the work is not vanishing, it is changing shape.

## Which VA tasks AI is taking

The jobs AI takes first have one thing in common: they are short, well defined, and text in, text out.

- **Repetitive writing and translation.** An Oxford study of freelance postings found demand for skills AI can substitute, such as writing and translation, fell 20 to 50% against the expected trend. The sharpest drop was in short jobs of one to three weeks.
- **Writing, translation and customer service postings.** An analysis of more than five million Upwork postings found most categories grew after ChatGPT, with three exceptions: writing, translation and customer service. Writing jobs fell 33% and translation 19%.
- **Low-level gig work.** Kalibrr's CEO expects AI to keep automating lower-level tasks, and says the gig jobs that remain will need high skills or fluency in the latest AI tools.
- **Single-task roles.** In VA Masters' study of 323 roles from 253 companies, only 26% of VA positions were focused on a single task. The agency's CEO put it simply: "AI wins on speed and simple, repeatable tasks."

None of these is a whole job. They are tasks inside jobs, and the VA who did only those tasks is the one at risk.

## Where the work is moving

The same studies that show tasks disappearing show bigger, broader roles appearing.

- **One VA, several functions.** Nearly three in four VA roles in the VA Masters study covered two or three business functions, averaging 2.02 each. Admin and operational support was still the largest category, at 37.9%, but it was usually paired with CRM management, social media marketing or lead generation.
- **AI as a job requirement.** 23.9% of the 322 openings studied required AI skills. The rate was highest in analytics and software at 44%, writing and content at 43.3%, design at 36.7% and social media at 35.6%.
- **Automation over chat.** Zapier and n8n appeared in more of those openings combined than ChatGPT alone. Clients are not paying VAs to chat with AI. They are paying them to wire it into the business.
- **Specialist titles.** Executive admin support is still popular, but the same agency's industry report found steady demand for CRM platform managers, technical support agents, email copywriters and online store managers.
- **Bigger projects.** A November 2026 study by OpenAI and Upwork found AI-related projects had larger fixed-price budgets and more hours of work, and combined tasks that had rarely appeared together before. The task mix in those postings became 45% more diverse between early 2023 and late 2025.
- **New roles in the BPO sector.** Industry leaders now list AI trainers, prompt engineers and AI maintenance specialists among the new roles being created.

## What the redirection looks like in practice

Take a typical VA's week and the pattern is clear. AI takes the first pass; the paid human work moves to the decisions around it. This table is my reading of the shift, not measured data.

| Old VA task | What AI or automation does now | Where the paid work moved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inbox and calendar triage | Sorts mail, drafts replies, proposes meeting slots | Deciding what matters, handling sensitive replies |
| Data entry | Extracts data from forms and syncs apps through Zapier or n8n | Building and fixing the automation, checking the exceptions |
| Blog and social copy | Writes first drafts | Brand voice, fact-checking, content plans, approvals |
| Tier 1 customer support | A chatbot answers routine questions | Escalations, refunds, upset customers, keeping the bot's knowledge current |
| Lead lists | Enrichment tools build the list | Qualifying leads, personal outreach, keeping the CRM clean |
| Translation | Machine translation | Review, localisation, accuracy where mistakes are costly |

In every row the task got cheaper, and the responsibility stayed with a person. That is the job now.

## The honest risks

Redirection is not painless, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

- **The bottom rung is shrinking.** Short, low-value jobs are hit first. A Wharton study of millions of freelance records found freelancers submitted 51 to 62% fewer bids after ChatGPT launched, and those in the most exposed fields shifted away from high-value jobs toward mid- and low-value ones.
- **Earnings dip before they recover.** Research cited by Brookings found freelancers in the most AI-exposed occupations saw 2% fewer contracts and 5% lower earnings after the new AI tools arrived.
- **More revenue, fewer seats.** IBPAP itself says revenue per worker will matter more as clients demand specialised, knowledge-intensive work. In its slow-growth scenario, revenue still rises to $43.3 billion while employment falls from 1.89 million to 1.85 million.
- **Beginners lose their training ground.** New VAs used to learn on simple tasks: data entry, scheduling, basic writing. If AI does those, the path into the industry gets steeper. That last point is my reading, not a measured trend.

## My stand

AI is not killing VA jobs. It is killing the old VA job description: the one where you wait for instructions, do one task, and hand it back. A VA who only does that is now competing with a monthly AI subscription. A VA who runs the systems is worth more than before.

I see this from the other side. [I build the automations](/services/) that take tasks off people's plates. For Spade Security Services, [AI agents](/labs/ai-agents-pilots-versus-production/) write blog articles and LinkedIn content, and n8n workflows move data between systems. None of it runs itself. Someone has to design the workflow, set the rules, check the output, catch the failures and update everything when the business changes. That someone is exactly the role a good VA can grow into.

My test is simple. **Your VA work is safe for now** when most of these are true:

- Your work involves judgment calls the client cannot write down as rules.
- You own a system end to end, such as a CRM, an online store or an inbox, not just one task inside it.
- You can build or fix an automation in Zapier, Make or n8n.
- You check and correct AI output, not just produce it.
- The client trusts you with customers, money or reputation.

**Redirect now** if most of your day is copy-paste, data entry, first-draft writing, basic translation or scheduling. Learn one automation tool properly, go deep on one CRM, and pick a niche such as e-commerce, real estate, healthcare admin or security operations.

**If you hire VAs**, do not replace them with a chatbot. Give them the AI tools, a small automation budget and a bigger scope. The VA who already knows your business is the cheapest person to run your AI.

## The larger point

The VA industry was itself a redirection. Work that used to sit in offices in the US, UK and Canada moved to homes in Laguna, Cebu and Davao because the internet made it possible. AI is the next move: from doing the tasks to running the systems that do them. The useful question is not whether AI will take VA jobs. It is whether you will be the person the AI works for, or the task it replaced.
